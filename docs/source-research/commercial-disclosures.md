# Commercial, platform and corporate-disclosure source landscape

Research date: 2026-09-08. This is a design review only: no authentication, connection test, download, subscription or production change was performed. The companion catalogue contains 34 named products. “Documented” means that a relevant first-party page was opened; it does not mean the proposed use is licensed or technically proven.

## Portfolio readout

The products fall into four different acquisition regimes, and the distinction matters more than the common word “API.” Official filing and identifier systems expose public records for statutory or public-good reasons. Commercial market-data vendors expose contract-defined observations. Social platforms expose limited functionality for an app, an authorized account or an approved research project. Community projects publish reusable knowledge under explicit licences, usually with attribution, share-alike or item-level qualifications. A single ingestion policy would therefore be unsafe.

Six candidates deserve first review for a commercially intended data foundation: SEC EDGAR, GLEIF, Wikidata, OpenStreetMap, Wikimedia project data and Crossref. Their first-party documentation exposes operational APIs or bulk routes and relatively clear public or open-data terms. Even these are conditional. SEC filing packages can include third-party material; GLEIF relationship exceptions cannot be treated as negative ownership facts; Wikidata assertions need references and ranks; Wikimedia media licences vary by item; OSM’s ODbL has database attribution and share-alike duties; and Crossref abstracts can remain copyrighted.

Companies House, ESEF national filing mechanisms, Polish ESPI/EBI disclosures, OpenAlex, GDELT and Our World in Data belong in the next review tier. They have high analytical value, but one of three pieces is incomplete: a cross-product reuse rule, a stable bulk interface, or documented historical completeness. Publicly viewable reports should be catalogued while legal and technical access is resolved. HTML availability is evidence of human access, not permission to build a permanent commercial mirror.

Commercial financial products are candidates for procurement, not the initial free/open intake. Marketstack, Alpha Vantage, Financial Modeling Prep and Nasdaq Data Link can accelerate multicountry coverage, but the licence attaches to the plan or individual dataset. GPW, TGE and GPW Benchmark are closer to the Polish source and therefore preferable for authoritative semantics, yet their display, redistribution and non-display clauses can be more operationally demanding. The design should compare exact instruments, latency, history, adjustment conventions, retention and derived-product rights before comparing price.

The platform APIs are not substitutes for worldwide social datasets. The YouTube Data API can look up public resources, but private data and actions require owner OAuth and the developer policy limits storage, audiovisual copying, aggregation and derived metrics. Instagram and LinkedIn primarily support accounts or organization pages that authorize the app. Reddit requires a separate agreement for commercial API use. X sells endpoint entitlements, with recent and archive search as different products. Meta Content Library and TikTok Research API are research programmes for qualified projects, not general commercial feeds. TikTok’s Commercial Content API concerns advertising transparency, not all organic content. These boundaries should appear in product requirements and customer-facing claims.

Polish coverage has two separate chains. ESPI and EBI carry issuer reports; PAP Biznes/InfoStrefa-style pages and issuer pages disseminate or mirror them. GPW market data carries trades, quotes, reference data and some index information. Those objects must not be collapsed. A filing is keyed by issuer and report identifier; a trade is keyed by instrument and exchange time. PAP pages may help discover a report but must not create a second filing fact. OTOMOTO explicitly combines its own platform supply and demand estimates with CEPiK, GUS and NBP information; the resulting report is a composite publication, not an independent observation to sum with each upstream series. Otodom’s published dashboards similarly describe platform listings and offer prices, not the whole housing stock or completed transaction market.

The proposed architecture should retain three times for every mutable source: the source’s effective or event time, its publication/update time when supplied, and Zohelo’s observation time. Raw filings and source files should be immutable. Current-state APIs should create snapshots only where their terms allow it. Incremental endpoints, `updated_at` filters, streams and delta files are different mechanisms: only GLEIF explicitly documents ready-to-use delta files among the strongest entity sources here. For other APIs, an update filter is a polling hint and must be backed by periodic reconciliation.

Entity resolution should prefer stable jurisdictional identifiers. CIK and accession number anchor SEC; company number anchors Companies House; LEI connects regulated legal entities; ISIN/MIC identify securities and venues; DOI, ORCID, ROR and ISSN connect scholarly entities; and Wikidata Q/P identifiers provide a community graph. Names, social handles and officer names are attributes, not universal keys. Every crosswalk should carry its source, confidence and validity period. Missing LEI parent data, an absent social account or a failed name match must remain “unknown,” not become a negative fact.

## Strong-source dossiers

### 1. SEC EDGAR APIs and archives

EDGAR offers the clearest filing ingestion pattern in this group. The [SEC EDGAR API page](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces) documents unauthenticated JSON for submissions and XBRL facts, real-time updates, and nightly bulk ZIP files. The [SEC developer resources](https://www.sec.gov/about/developer-resources) add comprehensive filing archives and daily or quarterly indexes and state the current fair-access ceiling of ten requests per second. This supports a bulk seed followed by accession-based updates without a paid vendor.

The main modeling risk is mistaking normalized company facts for the filing record. `companyfacts` combines facts across filings and contexts, while an accession identifies a filed package. Zohelo should preserve each original filing and its index metadata, then normalize facts with taxonomy, concept, unit, dimensions, period, decimals and filing accession. Restatements should add new facts or supersession links rather than overwrite history. Company names and tickers can change; CIK is the SEC anchor, with LEI/ISIN crosswalks added separately.

Fair access is an operational condition, not a performance target. Requests need an identifying user agent, caching and controlled concurrency. Filing documents can contain copyrighted exhibits, signatures and personal information. The initial review should define allowed forms, attachment handling and the boundary between factual extraction and redistribution of document content.

### 2. GLEIF Global LEI Index

GLEIF is the strongest global enterprise-linking candidate. Its [access and use page](https://www.gleif.org/en/lei-data/access-and-use-lei-data) describes a free, no-registration search of the complete LEI pool, full concatenated files, historical and current records, and Golden Copy delta files published three times daily. The [GLEIF API page](https://www.gleif.org/en/lei-data/gleif-api) documents legal-entity and ownership search plus mapped identifiers such as BIC and ISIN. Certified mappings also cover identifiers useful for linking securities, venues and OpenCorporates records.

An LEI record is narrower than a universal company register. Many enterprises do not need an LEI, and relationship reporting includes explicit exceptions. A missing parent record can mean an allowed reporting exception, unavailable information or no record; it must not be interpreted as “no parent.” Registration status and entity status are separate concepts. Legal name, address and jurisdiction change over time and should be modeled as effective-dated attributes.

The recommended load is a Golden Copy or concatenated-file seed, followed by idempotent delta application and periodic full reconciliation. Store Level 1, relationship records, reporting exceptions and mapping files as separate tables. Before onboarding, review the linked LEI Data Terms rather than relying only on the page’s “open” description, and select one canonical file format/version.

### 3. ESEF through national OAMs

ESEF is a reporting standard and national publication network, not a centralized ESMA filing database. ESMA’s [electronic reporting page](https://www.esma.europa.eu/issuer-disclosure/electronic-reporting) and [ESEF Reporting Manual](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/library/esma32-60-254_esef_reporting_manual.pdf) define XHTML and inline XBRL reporting requirements. Annual reports are filed with each country’s Officially Appointed Mechanism. A pan-European pipeline must therefore inventory OAMs and their interfaces, licences, retention and correction behavior country by country.

The technical prize is high: a filing package can contain machine-readable IFRS facts, human-readable statements and issuer extension taxonomies. The complexity is equally high. Facts depend on contexts, dimensions, units, scale/decimals and extension concepts anchored to the base taxonomy. A flattened “metric/year/value” table loses information and can silently combine consolidated, parent-only, segment and restated values. Store the original package and taxonomy, validation output, contexts and relationships before creating comparable gold measures.

Public filing availability does not automatically license redistribution of the complete annual report. OAM site terms and issuer document rights must be reviewed. The immediate deliverable should be an OAM matrix for Poland and priority countries, including package download route, identifiers, history depth and correction semantics. ESMA remains the standards authority in lineage; the national OAM remains the publication source.

### 4. Polish ESPI, EBI and PAP dissemination

The [KNF ESPI page](https://www.knf.gov.pl/dla_rynku/espi) and its [issuer-obligations guidance](https://www.knf.gov.pl/dla_konsumenta/kampanie_informacyjne/obowiazki_emitentow) establish the core boundary. ESPI carries regulated-market inside, current and periodic issuer reporting. EBI serves current, periodic and corporate-governance reporting for alternative trading-system issuers. [PAP Biznes](https://biznes.pap.pl/espi) presents ESPI/EBI report discovery and source-report links. The pages reviewed did not document a stable public API or complete bulk archive.

The source event should be identified by system, issuer, report number and publication timestamp, with each attachment preserved under that event. Corrections and supplements are later reports with explicit relationships, not edits to the original. An issuer can have obligations in more than one channel, so system name is part of the natural key. PAP, GPW company pages and issuer websites can all display the same report; they are distribution observations, not additional disclosures.

The practical next step is a bounded completeness study across a small issuer sample and date range. It should compare KNF/GPW/PAP-visible metadata and original attachments without assuming that the most convenient page is authoritative. Automated collection, attachment retention and republication rights need written or clearly published permission. Stooq is even less ready: its historical download pages were blocked during review, so its CSV pattern and terms remain catalogue-only.

### 5. YouTube Data API

The [YouTube Data API overview](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/getting-started) distinguishes API-key access to public resources from OAuth authorization for private data or user actions. That does not make public metadata an unrestricted historical dataset. The [Developer Policies](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/developer-policies) prohibit downloading audiovisual content without approval, restrict cross-owner aggregation and derived metrics, and require most stored non-authorized API data to be refreshed or deleted within thirty days. They also require deletion workflows and current-data display behavior. The [API Terms](https://developers.google.com/youtube/terms/api-services-terms-of-service) grant only the rights stated in the API agreement.

This creates a direct warehouse-design conflict. A conventional append-only snapshot of every public channel statistic could violate refresh and aggregation rules even though each lookup succeeded. Owner-authorized analytics can have different storage conditions, but the consent and authorization status must be checked. Zohelo should therefore keep this source outside the general lake until product counsel approves a specific API-client use case, a retention schedule and permitted transformations.

If approved, separate stable resource identifiers from changing statistics and store observed time plus authorization basis. Do not download thumbnails or video merely because metadata is available. Do not claim market-wide coverage: search is ranked, quota-consuming and not a historical census.

### 6. Meta Content Library and TikTok research/transparency products

Meta’s [Content Library announcement](https://about.fb.com/news/2023/11/new-tools-to-support-independent-research/) describes near-real-time access to selected public Facebook and Instagram content for qualified academic or nonprofit public-interest researchers through an ICPSR application. The API and user interface can support serious research, but download scope is limited. It is not a commercial social-listening entitlement and should not be presented as one.

TikTok separates two products with different purposes. The [Research API](https://developers.tiktok.com/products/research-api/) is for qualifying independent, noncommercial public-interest research in supported regions. The [Commercial Content API](https://developers.tiktok.com/products/commercial-content-api/) exposes advertising-transparency fields to approved applicants; its official page says current data availability is EU-focused and retains advertisement records for a bounded period after display. It is not an organic-post archive.

These sources should remain specialist entries. Approval belongs to a named project and institution, with security and ethics obligations. A research approval cannot be reused as a commercial data licence. Any resulting evidence store needs project separation, access logging, expiry handling and a plan for removed content. Instagram’s normal API is a different route again: it mainly serves professional accounts that authorize an app, not the public universe represented in a research library.

### 7. Wikidata and OpenStreetMap

Wikidata and OSM are powerful shared dimensions because both expose bulk and incremental routes with explicit licensing. The [Wikidata data-access guide](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Data_access) recommends dumps for large workloads, SPARQL for scoped graph queries and entity APIs for direct lookups. Its [copyright page](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Copyright) places structured data in the main structured namespaces under CC0, while other text remains CC BY-SA. Wikidata’s ranks, qualifiers and references must survive ingestion; selecting a single preferred value without its time or reference can turn a qualified community assertion into a false absolute.

The [OSM Planet documentation](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Planet.osm) provides weekly planet files, full-history data and minute/hour/day replication diffs, with archives reaching back to 2006. The [OSM copyright page](https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright) requires ODbL attribution and share-alike for the database. The public editing API, tiles and geocoder are operational services with separate usage policies; bulk analytics should use planet files or authorized extracts.

Both sources need bitemporal modeling. Wikidata statements and OSM geometry/tags change independently, and deletions or redactions can occur. They can link to official identifiers, but neither replaces the original registry or statistical source. The product must also decide whether its combined database triggers ODbL share-alike obligations before OSM is mixed with proprietary layers.

### 8. Crossref REST API

The [Crossref REST API documentation](https://www.crossref.org/documentation/retrieve-metadata/rest-api/) exposes member-deposited metadata for works, journals, publishers, funders and licences without basic signup. DOI is a strong work key and links can include ORCID, ISSN, funder identifiers and relations. Cursor-based retrieval and date filters make a historical seed plus overlapping update poll practical.

Crossref records what members deposited, not a canonical reconstruction of every publication. Dates can mean online, print, issued, created, deposited or indexed dates. Titles and author strings can change, and ORCID coverage is incomplete. Abstracts may be copyrighted even when bibliographic facts have little or no copyright restriction. The raw response should keep member, prefix, deposited/indexed timestamps, relation types and licence URLs.

Crossref heavily overlaps OpenAlex. Crossref should remain the DOI-registration metadata source; OpenAlex adds citation graphs, entity resolution and classifications. Differences are useful evidence, not duplicate rows to overwrite. Before production, measure Polish-language and Polish-publisher coverage, define whether abstracts are excluded, and confirm a courteous request identity and resumable cursor strategy.

## Missing coverage and decisions still required

No reviewed source supplies a legally reusable, complete historical census of public social content. No official public API or bulk feed was documented for ESPI/EBI/PAP report archives, Stooq, Otodom or OTOMOTO. Instagram first-party developer pages returned rate-limit errors during review, so that row is intentionally catalogue-only. Otodom pages returned access errors and are represented only by first-party search excerpts. OpenAlex’s redesigned help center confirmed API and data-access sections but did not expose current licence or snapshot terms in the opened material. GDELT calls itself free and open, yet the precise dataset licence and rights in underlying news articles remain unresolved.

The next decision should be a small eligibility gate, not a connection sprint. For each candidate, record the exact intended output: internal analytics, customer-visible facts, derived metrics, raw redistribution or document display. Then approve only the source/product/licence combinations that permit that output. This is especially important for financial market data, benchmark use, platform content and filing attachments. Once eligibility is settled, the first technical proofs should be SEC bulk-plus-incremental, GLEIF Golden Copy plus deltas, and one community bulk source with its attribution obligations implemented end to end.
