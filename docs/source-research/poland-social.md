# Poland: official statistics, society, health, education, culture and agriculture

Research checked 2026-09-08. This section inventories 35 named Polish public-data products or coherent publication families. It is research and ingestion design, not a record of connection tests. “Public” below means viewable or downloadable without asserting that commercial redistribution is permitted. The JSON records product-specific evidence and marks unresolved rights explicitly.

## Portfolio view

Poland has a strong official-statistics spine and several unusually useful administrative-data services. GUS should be the anchor provider. Its Bank Danych Lokalnych (BDL), Dziedzinowe Bazy Wiedzy (DBW), STRATEG, thematic workbooks, population and agricultural censuses, and TERYT reference data are distinct products of one organization. They must keep separate product lineage but must not be modeled as separate providers. BDL is the strongest general first-wave source because its API documentation combines broad domain coverage, deep local geography, explicit history, quotas and reuse terms. TERYT is the essential versioned key system for administrative units, localities and streets. DBW can add classification depth that BDL lacks.

The most valuable non-GUS machine interfaces are NFZ’s health APIs and the RSPO/RAD-on public catalogues. NFZ documents resource structures and paging. The opened terms for its healthcare-services and contracts APIs, plus the legacy treatment-date API, prohibit modifying data. The current terms for pharmacy reimbursement and treatment-date v1.4 still need separate review. RSPO and RAD-on expose institution-rich public data, but their current access terms, quotas, history behavior and licence scope were not established from opened official pages. Their entity identifiers are still valuable candidates for a Polish institutional graph.

Many other agencies publish serious statistics primarily as workbooks or PDF series: ZUS, KRUS, CKE, CIE/SIO, GIS, NIZP PZH, MRPiPS, MRiRW/ZSRIR and KOWR. These can support ingestion, but each family needs a narrow parser and a file-version policy. Public HTML catalogues frequently omit explicit commercial reuse rights. Those omissions are treated as blockers rather than filled by inference.

## Strong dossier 1: GUS Bank Danych Lokalnych

[BDL’s official API documentation](https://api.stat.gov.pl/Home/BdlApi) says that the bank contains thousands of indicators about the economy, society and environment; its first data are from 1995. It covers Poland, NUTS units, voivodeships, powiats, gminas and, for some variables, statistical localities. The REST API exposes JSON and XML and organizes data through subjects, variables, units, measures, attributes, aggregation levels and years. Data can be requested either for one variable across many territorial units or for one unit across many variables. This organization is suitable for a durable fact table rather than screen scraping.

The documentation is unusually precise about anonymous and registered request windows, and it applies CC BY 4.0 to the API data. The JSON preserves the exact limits once, avoiding duplicate transcription here. These facts make BDL eligible for a review-first pilot once attribution wording and a conservative request budget are agreed.

The main engineering risk is semantic revision, not access. GUS says the data and variable resources are continually expanded, verified and corrected. A loader should preserve the attribute attached to each observation, the measure, variable metadata and the territorial-unit version. Recent years should be re-read on a schedule and compared with stored values; a changed value is a revision, not an event stream. Annual rates, shares and per-capita values must remain non-additive. BDL also mirrors or republishes figures available in DBW, STRATEG, censuses and sector releases. Source and definition metadata should decide precedence; matching numbers alone should never trigger blind deduplication.

## Strong dossier 2: TERYT as the territorial key backbone

The [TERYT database page](https://eteryt.stat.gov.pl/eteryt/rejestr_teryt/udostepnianie_danych/baza_teryt/baza_teryt.aspx?contrast=default) exposes browsing, searching, full downloads, update files and a web service. Its core dictionaries are TERC for administrative territorial units, SIMC for localities and ULIC for street names. The public search documentation shows that users can search units, localities and streets by official identifier and name. The [GUS API TERYT page](https://api.stat.gov.pl/Home/TerytApi?lang=en) describes a SOAP service available free to public-administration and commercial entities, although production credentials are required.

TERYT should be modeled as slowly changing reference data. TERC codes are structured from voivodeship, powiat, gmina and unit-type components, but concatenated codes are meaningful only within a valid time interval. SIMC is the locality key. ULIC identifies a street-name record and does not by itself identify a full address; it must be scoped through locality and address relationships. Names must never be treated as stable keys. A sound backfill uses an official full snapshot plus every applicable update file, followed by periodic reconciliation to a later full snapshot.

The open questions are material. The opened pages did not establish the first retained change file, exact update cadence, API quotas or an explicit redistribution licence for republising TERYT tables. Free access and commercial-service eligibility do not answer the redistribution question. Until those points are resolved, the bulk files are a review-first reference candidate, while SOAP onboarding should wait for an approved credential request. NUTS must remain a separate, versioned classification linked through official crosswalks rather than inferred from code prefixes.

## Strong dossier 3: DBW, STRATEG and GUS workbooks

The [DBW API announcement](https://stat.gov.pl/en/news/api-to-the-knowledge-databases%2C86%2C1.html) and [API documentation](https://api.stat.gov.pl/Home/DBWApi?lang=en) describe long time series at detailed classification levels across 31 thematic areas. The API site states CC BY 4.0. DBW is therefore a strong companion to BDL for national series or classification breakdowns that do not fit BDL’s local-data model. Before ingestion, the team should confirm the production-key workflow, endpoint paging and limits, then enumerate table metadata rather than hard-code a few current table identifiers.

STRATEG is attractive for public-policy targets and territorial indicators, but its present technical evidence is thinner. The [STRATEG portal](https://strateg.stat.gov.pl/) is public, while current API authentication, quotas and product-specific reuse wording were not recovered. STRATEG should remain review-next until those documents are opened. When onboarded, target values and observed values belong in separate measures; gaps and ratios are non-additive. The source field matters because STRATEG can carry BDL or Eurostat observations.

GUS thematic releases are a pragmatic third channel. For example, [Tourism in 2024](https://stat.gov.pl/obszary-tematyczne/kultura-turystyka-sport/turystyka/turystyka-w-2024-r-%2C1%2C22.html) offers an XLSX workbook in a ZIP file, and [Cultural Participation in 2024](https://stat.gov.pl/obszary-tematyczne/kultura-turystyka-sport/kultura/uczestnictwo-ludnosci-w-kulturze-w-2024-r-%2C6%2C4.html) includes XLSX tables and a precision-results file. These structured releases often preserve dimensions and footnotes that are not as accessible in portal APIs. Ingestion should whitelist stable publication families, retain every original workbook and checksum, and store sheet/table labels and footnotes with normalized cells. A portal-wide crawler would create high maintenance cost and unclear scope.

## Strong dossier 4: NFZ APIs

NFZ maintains a coherent [API catalogue](https://api.nfz.gov.pl/) covering healthcare-service statistics, pharmacy reimbursement, chemotherapy, drug programmes, treatment dates and contracts. The [healthcare-services API](https://api.nfz.gov.pl/app-stat-api-jgp/) exposes a table index and aggregate hospitalisation breakdowns by sex, age, admission and discharge type, contract product, ICD-9 procedure and ICD-10 diagnosis. These are published statistics, not patient records. The [contracts API](https://api.nfz.gov.pl/app-umw-api/) covers agreements made by regional NFZ branches from 2008, including plans, monthly plan detail, service products and providers. Its terms document a limit of ten requests per second per IP.

The [treatment-date documentation](https://api.nfz.gov.pl/app-itl-api/) describes provider-supplied records for benefits with waiting lists, including service provider, delivery place, number of people waiting at month end and first available treatment date. The opened version 1.3 page explicitly says it is obsolete and points to version 1.4. This is a warning against building from the legacy URL. Even in the current service, the first available date is a volatile point-in-time observation, while the queue count has a month-end reference. A loader must give each value an observation timestamp and must not sum possible duplicate places or confuse availability with completed care.

The principal blocker is product-specific legal wording. The opened [healthcare-services terms](https://api.nfz.gov.pl/app-stat-api-jgp/terms) and [contracts terms](https://api.nfz.gov.pl/app-umw-api/terms) require attribution, ban overload and data modification, while the opened [treatment-date terms](https://api.nfz.gov.pl/app-itl-api/terms) apply to the obsolete v1.3 service. Legal review must determine whether ordinary normalization or derived metrics fall within those clauses and must separately verify current v1.4 and pharmacy-reimbursement terms. Provider linkage is another risk: NFZ provider identifiers, RPWDL register numbers and REGON identify different entity layers and should remain side by side until a defensible crosswalk is established.

## Strong dossier 5: epidemiology and health-system publications

[NIZP PZH’s EPIMELD index](https://wwwold.pzh.gov.pl/oldpage/epimeld/index_p.html) is a high-value historical public-health source. It lists fortnightly infectious-disease notifications from 1996 through 2026, annual *Choroby zakaźne i zatrucia w Polsce* bulletins from at least 1999 onward, annual vaccination bulletins, influenza notices, HIV/AIDS information and versioned surveillance case definitions. The index also records corrections to some annual bulletins. This history and explicit definition archive make EPIMELD stronger than a current dashboard for trend research.

The data are PDF-led. A first implementation should ingest annual bulletins and definitions, then assess whether fortnightly parsing adds enough value. Notifications are not necessarily unique people, and some notices are cumulative. The parser must capture whether a column is period-only or cumulative, the geographic unit, disease label, code and case-definition version. Published corrections should form new versions rather than silently overwrite the initial release. No explicit reuse licence was found, so rights remain unresolved despite public downloads.

GIS’s [State Sanitary Condition report](https://www.gov.pl/web/gis/raport---stan-sanitarny-kraju) is useful for food, environmental-health and inspection aggregates that are scarce elsewhere. It is built from national, voivodeship, powiat and border sanitary inspectorates. This creates an overlap trap: regional reports may feed the national report and cannot be added to it. The CeZ [research and data catalogue](https://ezdrowie.gov.pl/portal/home/badania-i-dane) adds dashboards for medical events from July 2021, e-prescriptions from 2020, e-referrals and selected prevention programmes. CeZ’s page presents these for public use but states “all rights reserved” and does not document a common bulk export. These dashboards are conditional candidates until machine-readable export and reuse permissions are established.

## Strong dossier 6: education and graduate outcomes

The [RSPO API](https://api.rspo.gov.pl/) and [public institution search](https://rspo.gov.pl/institutions) are the preferred school-entity source. RSPO integrates SIO data and supports finding establishments by attributes such as location and governing body. The RSPO number should be the primary school/establishment key. REGON can help link a governing or legal body, but it may be shared and is not a substitute for an RSPO establishment identifier. The model should distinguish institution, site, governing body, school type, status and validity dates. The remaining blockers are current API specification, authentication, quotas, licence and history behavior.

CIE’s [archival SIO statistics](https://cie.gov.pl/archiwalne-dane-statystyczne/) cover pupils, preschool education, special educational needs, foreign/minority languages, graduates and teachers. These public aggregate files can support annual school-year facts without exposing pupil records. Education reforms and changed school types cause real classification breaks; the ingestion model must retain school year, form definition and category labels rather than force all years into a single modern taxonomy.

At higher-education level, RAD-on exposes POL-on public data and reports. Institution and study-programme endpoints are more appropriate for initial ingestion than researcher/person resources. [ELA](https://ela.nauka.gov.pl/pl) is a distinct derived product: it combines ZUS and POL-on data to report graduate employment and earnings shortly after graduation and for up to five years. Only privacy-protected cohort aggregates are public. Relative earnings and unemployment indicators depend on local benchmarks and cannot be interpreted as simple currency or headcount measures. CKE’s [eighth-grade result archive](https://cke.gov.pl/egzamin-osmoklasisty/wyniki/) provides annual national results, distributions, location-based results and percentile scales for 2019–2026. Preliminary and final releases must remain distinguishable.

## Strong dossier 7: labour, culture, heritage and agriculture

For labour, the public-employment system should supply registered-unemployment stocks, flows and vacancy statistics once the canonical current workbook archive is pinned down. These concepts differ from survey-based unemployment. Stocks at month end cannot be added across months, and vacancies, new registrations and removals are separate flows. [Barometr Zawodów](https://barometrzawodow.pl/en/module/forecasts-on-maps) supplies annual expert shortage/balance/surplus assessments by occupation and powiat, nationwide from 2016. It is a forecast classification, not a vacancy count. Its occupation grouping must be versioned.

For culture, GUS annual institution tables and participation surveys are stronger analytical sources, while NID adds actual heritage entities. The official open-data catalogue for NID’s [immovable-monument inventory](https://dane.gov.pl/pl/dataset/2627) confirms a nationwide dataset created under heritage law. It should be treated as an inventory/compilation, not conclusive proof of present legal status; provincial conservator registers may be more current. Addresses of privately owned heritage assets also deserve a narrow allowlist even when records are public.

For agriculture, the [ZSRIR bulletin page](https://www.gov.pl/web/rolnictwo/biuletyny-informacyjne) says that releases moved to a new portal from issue 1/2025. This creates a source migration that must be modeled explicitly. Weekly and monthly price observations need commodity quality, market stage, region, tax basis, unit and currency. [KOWR grain reports](https://www.gov.pl/web/kowr/Rynek-zboz) and meat reports are frequent and useful but often derive from ZSRIR or other official sources; the upstream source should be stored to prevent duplicate observations. ARiMR programme statistics are potentially valuable for CAP applications, decisions and payments, but no single official aggregate catalogue with stable structure was confirmed. Named aggregate series should be chosen before onboarding, and personal beneficiary lists should remain outside the initial scope.

## Coverage gaps and onboarding order

The inventory is strongest for territorial indicators, censuses, social insurance, hospital utilisation, infectious-disease surveillance, school/higher-education institutions, exam outcomes, cultural institutions, tourism establishments and agricultural market prices. The main gaps are not the absence of official statistics but weak machine access or unclear reuse terms. Social-assistance reports are fragmented across programmes and legislative regimes. ARiMR aggregate programme reporting lacks an evidenced unified catalogue. RPWDL is publicly searchable, but a documented bulk/API route and a safe public-field policy were not established. CeZ dashboards are analytically useful but lack verified export and reuse provisions. Sports data were not promoted to a separate row because no stable, machine-readable official product family was evidenced strongly enough; relevant participation/facility indicators may already exist in BDL and GUS thematic publications.

The recommended sequence is: (1) BDL and a TERYT bulk-file proof of concept after TERYT reuse clarification; (2) DBW and selected structured GUS workbooks; (3) NIZP PZH annual bulletins and SIO/CKE workbooks; (4) NFZ aggregate APIs after legal review of the no-modification clause; (5) RSPO and RAD-on institution/programme resources after current API and licence review; and (6) PDF-heavy or fragmented specialist families. REGON BIR should be treated as a controlled enrichment service, not a harvesting source. The initial field set should exclude personal records and minimize contact/address details for sole proprietors, medical practices, researchers and private heritage owners.
